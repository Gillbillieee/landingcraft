How to Write a Resume With No Experience in 2026

The Challenge: According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, approximately 30% of recent college graduates are underemployed or working in jobs that don't require a degree.[1] Breaking into your first professional role requires a strategic approach to resume writing.

Why This Matters

Your resume is often the first impression you make on an employer. Without traditional work experience, you need to highlight other aspects of your background that demonstrate value.

Key Strategies for Writing a Resume With No Experience

1. Use a Functional Resume Format

Instead of chronological format (which emphasizes work history), use a functional format that highlights skills and achievements. This shifts focus from when you worked to what you can do.

2. Highlight Education and Relevant Coursework

List relevant courses, projects, and academic achievements. If you scored well in subjects related to the job, mention them specifically.

3. Include Volunteer Work and Extracurriculars

Volunteer positions, club memberships, and extracurricular activities demonstrate leadership, teamwork, and commitment — all qualities employers value.

4. Showcase Projects and Portfolio Work

Academic projects, personal projects, or freelance work can substitute for professional experience. Include links to GitHub, Behance, or other portfolio sites.

5. Emphasize Transferable Skills

Skills from part-time jobs, internships, or even hobbies (like managing a gaming community) can translate to workplace competencies like communication, problem-solving, and project management.

Resume Sections to Include

  1. Contact Information: Name, phone, email, LinkedIn profile
  2. Objective Statement: Brief statement about your career goals and what you bring to the role
  3. Education: Degree, school, graduation date, GPA (if 3.5+)
  4. Relevant Skills: Technical skills, languages, software proficiency
  5. Projects: Academic or personal projects with descriptions
  6. Volunteer/Extracurricular Activities: Roles and responsibilities

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving large employment gaps without explanation
  • Including irrelevant personal information (age, marital status)
  • Using generic objective statements that don't mention the specific role
  • Forgetting to tailor your resume for each application
