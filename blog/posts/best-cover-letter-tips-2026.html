Best Cover Letter Tips for 2026 — What Gets You Hired

The Reality: According to a 2025 survey by Jobvite, 77% of recruiters and hiring managers read cover letters as part of the evaluation process.[1] Yet many candidates still submit generic, copy-pasted cover letters that don't stand out.

Why Cover Letters Still Matter in 2026

While some companies have moved to application-only processes, a well-crafted cover letter can be the difference between getting an interview and being rejected. It demonstrates your interest, communication skills, and ability to tailor your application.

Top Cover Letter Tips for 2026

1. Personalize Every Cover Letter

Never send a generic cover letter. Research the company, mention specific projects or values that resonate with you, and explain why you want to work there specifically.

2. Match Your Skills to the Job Description

Identify the top 3-5 requirements from the job posting and provide specific examples of how you've demonstrated each skill in previous roles or projects.

3. Quantify Your Achievements

Use numbers and metrics: "Increased social media engagement by 40% in 3 months" is far more compelling than "Improved social media performance."

4. Keep It Concise

Your cover letter should be one page, no longer than 3-4 paragraphs. Hiring managers spend an average of 7 seconds on initial resume scans.[2]

5. Show, Don't Tell

Instead of saying "I'm a great communicator," describe a specific situation where your communication skills solved a problem or achieved a result.

Cover Letter Format That Works

  1. Header: Your contact info, date, employer contact info
  2. Greeting: Address by name if possible ("Dear Ms. Johnson")
  3. Opening paragraph: State the position and why you're excited
  4. Body paragraphs (2-3): Highlight relevant achievements and skills
  5. Closing paragraph: Reiterate interest and request an interview

Common Cover Letter Mistakes

  • Repeating your resume word-for-word
  • Using overly formal or casual language
  • Making it all about what you want, not what you offer
  • Ignoring typos and grammatical errors
